**Real-Time EEG for robot control and neurofeedback applications**  
The students Raúl García Luengo and Rodrigo Quispe Avila, under the supervision of Guiomar Niso, presented their work in a poster at CajalXmas 2024. The research focuses on advancing neurofeedback and Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) technology. By utilizing real-time EEG data, deep learning models, and an intuitive interface, this platform facilitates brainwave-based control of external devices, such as robotic arms and cognitive training tools.  
This study, in collaboration with Neurosity Crown EEG technology, explores how neurofeedback can improve cognitive function and potentially offer therapeutic benefits for conditions such as ADHD, stroke rehabilitation, and mental training. The integration of AI and real-time processing brings BCI technology closer to practical, everyday applications.  
This research underscores the growing potential of BCI systems to create new possibilities in neurotechnology and assistive devices.

Epilepsy: [CajalXmas_2024_Poster_epilepsy_CS.pdf](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1rytJx6vV3-m8JDYfj7IE7CvmNbevAS81/view?usp=drive_link)  
**Detection of high frequency oscillations correlated with interictal spikes as a biomarker of epilepsy**  
The students Cristina Salinas and Marta Navarro, under the supervision of Guiomar Niso and with the collaboration of Rafael Toledano and Paloma Parra from Hospital Ruber Internacional presented their poster at CajalXmas 2024. Their study investigates the potential of EEG biomarkers, specifically High-Frequency Oscillations (HFOs), in improving epilepsy diagnostics. The research focuses on the use of HFOs to identify the Seizure Onset Zone (SOZ), a critical factor in epilepsy treatment.  
Key findings from the study include:

* HFOs are more prevalent in clinically significant spikes, making them a promising marker for diagnosis.  
* Detecting HFOs improves the accuracy of SOZ mapping, which is crucial for effective surgical treatment.

This research contributes to the development of non-invasive and automated methods for epilepsy diagnosis, with the potential to enhance treatment outcomes for patients globally. The study highlights how HFO analysis could play an important role in improving the precision of epilepsy care.

Ageing: [CajalXmas_2024_Poster_ageing_CP.pdf](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1B8oeg2jVg8Fbd0ObqnZw3BnwF8bg3QW_/view?usp=drive_link)  
**Decoding the Aging Brain: EEG Biomarkers for Alzheimer’s Disease**  
How does brain activity change with age, and can we detect early signs of Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) using EEG? The CajalXmas 2024 project investigates these crucial questions by analyzing spectral power and fractal dimensions of EEG signals.  
By studying healthy aging versus Alzheimer’s patients, researchers observed distinct patterns:  
🔹 Slower EEG frequencies in AD patients, indicating cognitive decline.  
🔹 Increased neural noise and irregularity with age, captured through fractal analysis.  
These findings suggest that EEG-based biomarkers could enhance early diagnosis and complement existing biological markers like amyloid plaques and tau proteins. With Alzheimer’s affecting over 50 million people worldwide, developing accessible and non-invasive diagnostic tools is more important than ever.  
Could EEG analysis revolutionize the way we detect and treat neurodegenerative diseases?
